 Purpose and scope
 Evidence supporting Q4 of the TikTok Shop Data Security and Privacy Review. The materials document
 multi-factor authentication for three identified administrative service accounts and technical workstation
 password and locking controls. The screenshots demonstrate account-level settings only; they do not establish
 organization-wide enforcement or the configuration of any other privileged accounts.


 Summary of confirmed controls

   Control                                Evidence / verification


   GitHub MFA                             Authenticator app configured; passkeys shown (Exhibit A)


   Supabase MFA                           Microsoft Authenticator registered on 30 Oct 2026 (Exhibit B)


   Vercel 2FA                             Two-Factor Authentication Active; TOTP enrolled (Exhibit C)


   Ubuntu screen lock                     GNOME: lock-enabled=true; lock-delay=0; idle-delay=300 seconds


   Ubuntu password                        Account password set; pam_pwquality 1.4.5; yescrypt in PAM


   Password policy                        minlen=12, minclass=3, maxrepeat=3, dictcheck=1, enforcing=1


   Functional tests                       Weak sample rejected for length; strong sample pwscore=100


   Idle locking                           User reported successful automatic lock after five minutes



 Limitations and pending confirmations
 The password-strength tests exercise libpwquality, not the entire PAM password-change workflow. A password
 prompt on unlock was not separately confirmed in the provided conversation. The account has no periodic
 password expiration; the formal policy rationale remains to be approved. Recovery-code storage, coverage for
 other privileged administrators, and formal approval remain unverified. Screenshots are user-provided and do
 not constitute an independent platform audit.


 Screenshot handling
 Exhibits A–C are the supplied screenshots. A personal email address visible in Exhibit C has been redacted;
 other incidental account identifiers remain visible. Do not attach recovery codes, authentication secrets, or
 passwords.
